# Study 0002: favorite-longshot bias on settled Kalshi binary markets, take two

Second bounded research cycle on the same hypothesis as study 0001
(`research/studies/0001-favorite-longshot/`), registered per issue #68 after study 0001 came back
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE with zero analysable markets (`docs/research/graveyard.md`, 2026-09-27). The
frozen plan is `REGISTRATION.md` and `config.json`, committed and pushed
(`e000389b9b82c9d94169f6dcf09e01a96b42eace`, 2026-09-28T18:12:37Z) before any settled outcome was
fetched through the budgeted client. Results are in `RESULTS.md` and `results.json`, generated by
code; the verdict and the evidence are summarized here and in the PR.

Unlike study 0001, this run produced a real, analysable sample: 976 markets on 165 events, meeting
the registered minimum of 100 pooled-bin events. The verdict is still **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**,
but for the ordinary statistical reason (a wide confidence interval straddling the minimum useful
effect), not a data-collection failure.

## What changed from study 0001

Study 0001 pooled every settled market across the whole exchange, one UTC day at a time, and every
day in its window turned out to have more than its 12-page cap of settled markets, so the
registered rule dropped every day whole. This run bounds the population to four specific series
(`KXHIGHNY`, `KXHIGHLAX`, `KXHIGHCHI`, `KXUSDJPY`) chosen from `GET /series?include_volume=true`
metadata and sized with `GET /events?series_ticker=...&status=settled` counts — genuinely
outcome-free, since `EventData` carries no result or price field — before the exact listing rule
was frozen. See `REGISTRATION.md` for the full sizing procedure, including the one disclosed
deviation (a handful of ad-hoc, unledgered schema probes that did touch a few markets' `result`
field before the registration commit, made to verify timestamp math, not to pick series by
outcome).

## Run report (2026-09-28): a real, analysable sample

Verdict: **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**. Hypothesis on this sample: **UNDETERMINED**. The primary
metric's 95% CI, `[-0.0106, 0.0137]`, straddles both zero and the (unverified) minimum useful
effect `0.0118` — too wide to reject or promote, not because the population was empty this time.

- Registration commit `e000389` at 2026-09-28T18:12:37Z, pushed the same minute. First
  settled-outcome request through the budgeted client at 2026-09-28T18:15:56Z
  (`data/catalog/study-0002.json`, `first_settled_outcome_fetch_at`). VERIFIED.
- Public endpoints worked without credentials: all 1066 of 1066 requests returned 200, zero
  retries, zero errors (`requests.jsonl`, ledger sha256
  `82748e3dc4f7b9dbcfb925b708c8361519d869b74f7f3703e792a8f4a2a9f1fb` in the catalog) — well inside
  the 2000-request budget, with headroom of over 900 requests unused.
- Listing: 9 requests found all four series' full settled history in the window in one page each
  (`max_pages_per_series` 50 was never approached); no series was dropped. 1494 markets listed,
  1039 eligible after the registered per-market filters (excluded: 389 `opened_after_decision`, 66
  `zero_volume`; zero markets excluded for closing before their decision time, unlike the concern
  that motivated tracking that reason in study 0001).
- Trades: all 1039 eligible markets were sampled (`sampling_stopped: "sample exhausted"`, no
  budget-driven cutoff), each requiring exactly one or two trade-window requests. 976 of 1039
  produced a usable feature row (48 excluded for no trade in the 24 h window, 15 for window volume
  below the 10-contract minimum), on 165 distinct events — above the registered minimum of 100.
- **One assumption in REGISTRATION.md turned out wrong, harmlessly:** the design said "no
  `/historical/trades` calls are needed" because the whole settlement window is after the cutoff.
  That is true for settlement, but a market's *price window* starts 24 h before its decision time,
  and for the earliest few markets in the window (settling within a day of the 2026-07-30 cutoff)
  part of that price window falls before the cutoff. `trade_windows()` (copied unmodified from
  study 0001) already splits such a window across `/historical/trades` and `/markets/trades`
  correctly — this is exactly the case it exists for — so no code changed and no result is
  affected; only the prose assumption in REGISTRATION.md was wrong, and REGISTRATION.md is not
  edited after outcomes are seen, so it is corrected here instead.
- **Disclosed limitation:** 359 of 1039 trade-window responses hit the 1000-row page limit
  (`validation.trade_pages_truncated_at_limit` in `results.json`). Every query already bounds
  `max_ts` at the decision time and returns newest-first, so the specific trade used for price (the
  newest at or before the decision) is unaffected; only the `window_volume` diagnostic can be
  undercounted for very active markets, and only in the direction that could make an already-kept
  market look less liquid than it is, never the reverse — it cannot manufacture a false pass of
  the 10-contract minimum.
- Fee coefficients are still UNVERIFIED (fee schedule PDF blocked again); the decision rule treats
  fees exactly as study 0001's frozen rule specified for this case, so PROMOTE was not reachable
  this run regardless of the CI.
- The secondary recalibration candidate found essentially no effect (`a = 0.9934`, held-out Brier
  improvement `-0.0000`): on this sample there is no evidence the one-parameter logit rescaling
  beats the raw market price.

Next action: this line of inquiry (favorite-longshot bias on Kalshi's mutually-exclusive daily
ladders) now has one honest, complete, inconclusive cycle. Revisiting it productively needs either
a larger or lower-variance sample (more series, or a longer settlement window, both of which fit
comfortably in the unused request budget from this run) or a different mechanism hypothesis;
simply re-running the same four series over a longer window is the cheapest next step if this is
revisited.

## Reproducibility

Status (2026-09-28): **not reproducible from a clean clone; reproducible from the preserved
snapshot**, the same status as study 0001, for the same reason (no approved shared data store yet;
issue #69 is still open).

- What exists: the committed `results.json`
  (sha256 `444f578425cb802ec17428a55f0feb17c5e7d938b63b9cf22c60cde3f4ee67dc`), `RESULTS.md`, and the
  catalog `data/catalog/study-0002.json` with the sha256 of all 1065 raw files and of the request
  ledger `requests.jsonl`.
- What is missing: the raw files themselves. They are gitignored under `data/raw/study-0002/` and
  archived outside git at `~/Desktop/ffl-artifacts/study-0002-snapshot.tar.gz` (sha256 in the
  paired `.sha256` file next to it), the same pattern study 0001 used.
- Acquiring the snapshot: extract the archive into `data/raw/study-0002/`. `just study-0002` then
  checks every file and the ledger against the catalog before analysing anything; a single
  mismatch, missing file or uncatalogued file stops it with exit 2 and leaves `results.json`
  untouched.
- Re-fetching: `cd research && uv run python studies/0002-favorite-longshot-v2/fetch.py` (spends up
  to the request budget) produces a new snapshot. Kalshi's listings and the request ledger change
  over time, so a re-fetch will not match the catalog; `fetch.py` then reports DIFFERENT SNAPSHOT
  and exits 1. A different snapshot is a different experiment and needs its own registration and
  trial; it is never evidence about this run.
