# ADR-0003: Venue selection

## Status

Proposed. Date proposed: 2026-09-27. Not yet approved by a human.

**This ADR does not choose a venue.** The operators' legal jurisdiction is unknown, and several candidate venues refuse US residents. Every eligibility statement below is marked **REQUIRES HUMAN CONFIRMATION** until the operators confirm where they, and the entity that will trade, are resident and organized, and eligibility is confirmed.

## Context

The initial arena is liquid crypto perpetuals and spot, with software-only execution co-regioned with the venue (§1). The candidates named for review are Binance, Bybit, OKX, Deribit, Coinbase (including Coinbase International Exchange and Coinbase Derivatives), Kraken, Hyperliquid and CME crypto futures. For each one this ADR records who the venue refuses to serve, the quality of its API, its fee tiers, its market-maker program, its historical data and where its matching engine runs.

Research was done on 2026-09-27. Some official pages could not be fetched (HTTP 403, timeouts, or pages that render only in a browser). Where a figure comes from a search-result snippet or a third-party site rather than the venue's own page, it says so. A matching-engine region is stated only where a source says so; otherwise it is "unconfirmed". Fees change often and must be re-checked before any decision rests on them. None of this is legal advice.

### API, fees, programs, data and matching engine

**Binance (binance.com).** REST, WebSocket API, WebSocket streams, FIX and SBE market data ([limits](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/rest-api/limits)). Limits are IP-weighted; exceeding them returns HTTP 429 and repeat violations bring automatic bans from 2 minutes to 3 days ([general info](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/rest-api/general-api-information)). FIX market-data sessions allow 2000 messages per 60 seconds and require Ed25519 keys ([FIX docs](https://github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/blob/master/fix-api.md)). Spot fees are 0.100% maker and taker at VIP0 and 0.011% / 0.023% at VIP9; USD-M futures taker is 0.095% at VIP0 ([fee schedule](https://www.binance.com/en/fee/schedule)). Maker rebates come through the spot and futures market-maker programs ([spot MM](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/binance-introduces-maker-fee-rebates-to-the-spot-market-maker-program-777646dadd8846dab8a84ce32c9b7cc4), [futures MM](https://www.binance.info/en/support/faq/detail/b65fefd0fee84893ad946dc6f707dedc)). Historical klines, trades, aggTrades, bookTicker and depth are published at [data.binance.vision](https://data.binance.vision/). Matching engine: an AWS blog says "Binance in Tokyo" ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/ultra-low-latency-cross-region-crypto-trading-with-avelacom-and-aws/)); no Binance source confirms the region, so **unconfirmed by the venue**.

**Binance.US.** Spot only. 0% maker / 0.02% taker, 0.01% taker at the top volume tier, no maker rebates ([fees](https://www.binance.us/fees)); market-maker program with higher API limits ([MM program](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9842933-what-is-the-binance-us-market-maker-program)); API docs at [docs.binance.us](https://docs.binance.us/). Matching engine: unconfirmed.

**Bybit.** REST v5 and WebSocket; no native FIX found. 600 HTTP requests per 5 seconds per IP ([rate limits](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/rate-limit)); order book depth up to 1000 levels, level 1 pushed every 10 ms ([order book stream](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/websocket/public/orderbook)). VIP0 spot 0.10% / 0.10% and perps 0.02% / 0.055% (third party, [datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/bybit-vip-levels-explained); the official [fee page](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Trading-Fee-Structure) timed out). Maker rebates up to -0.01% through the market-maker incentive program ([MM program](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-the-Market-Maker-Incentive-Program), search snippet). Historical data at [bybit.com history-data](https://www.bybit.com/derivatives/en/history-data) (search snippet). Matching engine: the AWS blog above says "Bybit in Singapore"; **unconfirmed by the venue**.

**OKX.** REST and WebSocket; no FIX in the v5 docs. 1,000 order requests per 2 seconds per sub-account; US users connect through us.okx.com ([API docs](https://www.okx.com/docs-v5/en/)). Global Regular tier spot 0.08% / 0.10% and perps 0.02% / 0.05% (third party, [bitdegree](https://www.bitdegree.org/crypto/tutorials/okx-fees)). OKX US spot Regular tier 0.20% / 0.35%, with negative maker fees at VIP9 ([US fee framework](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/updates-to-us-fee-framework-2026)). Institutional program and Enhanced Liquidity Program ([institutional](https://www.okx.com/institutional), [ELP](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/okx-introduces-enhanced-liquidity-program)). Official historical data: tick trades from September 2021, L2 order book from March 2023 ([historical data](https://www.okx.com/en-us/historical-data)). Matching engine: OKX announced moving latency-sensitive services from Hong Kong to Tokyo, rescheduled to the end of July 2026 ([migration announcement](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/okx-trading-server-migration-announcement-hong-kong-tokyo)); the cloud provider and exact site are unconfirmed.

**Deribit.** JSON-RPC over WebSocket and HTTP, FIX, and the "Starbase" SBE order entry and multicast market data ([docs](https://docs.deribit.com/)); multicast also offered in AWS London and Tokyo ([announcement](https://insights.deribit.com/industry/deribit-extends-multicast-market-data-to-aws/)). The site now brands itself "Deribit by Coinbase" ([rate limits page](https://www.deribit.com/kb/deribit-rate-limits)). USDC perps 0.00% maker / 0.05% taker (search snippet of the [fees page](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944746248989-Fees), which returned 403). No public market-maker rebate program found; there is Market Maker Protection ([MMP](https://www.deribit.com/pages/information/MMP)). All trades are available from the history API ([historical trades](https://docs.deribit.com/articles/accessing-historical-trades-orders)). Matching engine: Equinix LD4, Slough, UK (search snippet of [server infrastructure](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944617582877-Server-Infrastructure), which was not fetched; consistent with the AWS blog's "Deribit in London").

**Coinbase Exchange / Advanced (spot).** REST (public 10 requests per second per IP, private 15 per second per profile; [rate limits](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/rest-api/rate-limits)), WebSocket with `full` and `level3` channels for L3 data ([channels](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/websocket-feed/channels)), and FIX market data and order entry ([FIX market data](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/fix-api/market-data)). No official bulk historical download found. Matching engine: Coinbase's postmortem of its 2026-05-07 outage is reported to place the matching engine in AWS us-east-1 ([postmortem](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/a-postmortem-of-our-may-7-2026-outage)); that page returned 403 on re-check, so treat the region as **reported, not re-verified**.

**Coinbase International Exchange.** FIX sessions are disconnected above 1000 messages per second ([rate limits](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/international-exchange/introduction/rate-limits-overview), search snippet). Perp fees 0 to 60 bps by tier, maker rebates at high tiers ([fees](https://help.coinbase.com/en/international-exchange/trading-deposits-withdrawals/international-exchange-fees), search snippet). Liquidity program page returned 403. Matching engine: unconfirmed.

**Coinbase Derivatives.** FIX 4.4 order entry, market data and drop copy; SBE; UDP market data by cross-connect; REST; AWS PrivateLink in us-east-1 and us-east-2 ([connectivity](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/derivatives/introduction/connectivity)). Fees "as low as 0.02%" ([launch post](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/perpetual-futures-have-arrived-in-the-us)); full schedule unconfirmed. **Kraken.** REST, WebSocket v2 including a Level 3 channel ([level3](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/websocket-v2/level3/)), and FIX for spot and futures ([FIX](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/guides/fix-auth/)); spot REST uses a decaying counter by verification tier ([spot REST limits](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/guides/spot-rest-ratelimits/)). Official CSV downloads of OHLCVT and time-and-sales ([OHLCVT](https://support.kraken.com/articles/360047124832-downloadable-historical-ohlcvt-open-high-low-close-volume-trades-data), [time and sales](https://support.kraken.com/articles/360047543791-downloadable-historical-market-data-time-and-sales-)). **Hyperliquid.** REST (info and exchange endpoints) and WebSocket; no FIX. 1200 weight per minute per IP, plus per-address limits tied to volume traded ([rate limits](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits)); the `l2Book` call returns at most 20 levels per side ([info endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint)). Historical data in requester-pays S3 buckets, updated roughly monthly with no guarantee ([historical data](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/historical-data)). **CME crypto futures.** Bitcoin, Ether, Micro Bitcoin and Micro Ether futures, financially settled on Globex ([micro crypto](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/cryptocurrencies/micro-cryptocurrency-futures-and-options.html)). iLink 3 (SBE) order entry and MDP 3.0 market data ([develop to Globex](https://www.cmegroup.com/solutions/market-access/globex/develop-to-globex.html)), with full-depth Market by Order ([MBO FAQ](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/faqs/market-by-order-mbo.html)). Market-maker programs for micro crypto futures are named in CFTC filings ([2024 rule filings](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-regulation/rule-filings/2024/cftc-rule-filings-2024.pdf), search snippet). Historical data through CME DataMine ([crypto data](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/browse-data/cryptocurrency-data.html)). CME trades only in its session hours, unlike the 24/7 crypto venues; the exact hours were not checked.

## Decision

1. **No venue is selected until the operators confirm their jurisdiction.** Phase 0 records candidates only. Recording, paper trading and any key creation wait on this.
2. **If the operators or the trading entity are US persons** (REQUIRES HUMAN CONFIRMATION), the shortlist is limited to venues that state they serve US clients: Coinbase Derivatives (US perpetual-style futures, Chicago), Kraken Derivatives US (perps on Bitnomial through NinjaTrader Clearing), CME crypto futures through an FCM, and for spot Coinbase Exchange, Kraken spot (not New York or Maine) and Binance.US (not the listed states). OKX US perps stay off the shortlist until the entity and eligibility are confirmed. Binance.com, Bybit, OKX global, Deribit, Coinbase International Exchange, Kraken Derivatives international and Hyperliquid are excluded.
3. **If the operators and entity are not US persons** (REQUIRES HUMAN CONFIRMATION of their actual jurisdiction against each venue's restricted list), the broader set opens: Binance and Bybit for perp liquidity and published historical data, OKX, Deribit, Coinbase International Exchange and Hyperliquid. Each still needs a check of that jurisdiction against the venue's own terms.
4. **Selection criteria, once eligibility is known,** in this order: legal eligibility for the actual entity; ability to create trade-only, IP-allowlisted API keys with withdrawal disabled (root rule 9; not yet researched per venue); official historical L2 or L3 data for backtesting; FIX or low-latency market data; a published market-maker program; and a matching-engine region that a source confirms, so the engine can be co-regioned.
5. **Downloading a venue's public historical data is not assumed to be allowed** for a person the venue refuses to serve. Whether the terms permit that is part of the human confirmation.

## Alternatives

- **Pick Binance now as the default for liquidity.** Rejected: the operators might be US persons, and Binance.com refuses US persons.
- **Pick a US-regulated venue now as the safe default.** Safer legally, but it may give up liquidity and data the operators are entitled to if they are not US persons. The choice should follow the facts about the operators.
- **Trade through a broker or aggregator.** Adds a counterparty and latency and hides the venue's order book mechanics that market making depends on. Not considered further for the initial arena.

## Consequences

- Recording and paper trading cannot start until a human confirms jurisdiction and picks a venue.
- The engine-core lane must keep the gateway venue-agnostic (§1), since the venue is open.
- If the operators are US persons, the available perpetual products are regulated US futures with different contract terms (for example Coinbase's five-year expiry with hourly funding), and the backtest fee and funding models must follow those terms.
- Several figures here come from search snippets or third-party sites. They must be re-verified on the venue's own pages before a decision relies on them.

## Review date

When the operators confirm their jurisdiction, and in any case before any API key is created or any recorder connects to a venue.
